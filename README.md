# Reconnect Scholarship Programs and Adult Community College Enrollment

## Overview

This repository contains the code used to evaluate adult community college enrollment trends following the implementation of Reconnect scholarship programs in Massachusetts, Michigan, and Tennessee.

## Research Question

Are last-dollar Reconnect scholarship programs associated with increases in adult community college enrollment, both individually within states and collectively across states?

## Data

1. Integrated Postsecondary Education Data System (IPEDS)
2. State Higher Education Agencies

## Methods

1. Difference-in-Differences (DiD)
2. Comparative Interrupted Time Series (CITS)
3. Newey-West Estimation
4. Wild Cluster Bootstrap Analysis
5. Parallel Trends and Placebo Tests

## Results

* Massachusetts Reconnect was associated with the largest enrollment gains among the states examined.
* Tennessee Reconnect produced positive short-term enrollment effects, though these gains were not sustained over the longer term.
* Michigan Reconnect appeared to stabilize enrollment during a period affected by broader pandemic-related enrollment declines.
* In a pooled analysis across Massachusetts, Michigan, and Tennessee, adult enrollment increased by approximately 164 students during the implementation year and by approximately 358 students one year after implementation.
* Wild Cluster Bootstrap inference suggested that the year-after effect was statistically significant, while the immediate effect was not.
* Comparative Interrupted Time Series and placebo analyses generally supported the validity of the findings.
